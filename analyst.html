GM

What's the probability the Strait of Hormuz closes to tanker traffic in the next 30 days, and what should we do about our Gulf-bound charters?

GH

Daybreak estimates a 41% probability of a significant disruption to tanker traffic through the Strait of Hormuz in the next 30 days — meaning a partial closure, a mining or seizure incident, or a war-risk designation severe enough to halt insured transits. That's up from 35% a week ago.

Three drivers are pushing this higher:

  • Two additional IRGC fast-boat intercepts logged this week, versus a trailing average of one every ten days.
  • War-risk insurance quotes on Gulf transits have widened for the first time in six weeks.
  • A U.S. carrier group extension was announced, which historically correlates with elevated (not reduced) short-term risk in our model.

For your Gulf-bound charters, we'd suggest reviewing war-risk coverage limits on any vessel scheduled to transit in the next two weeks, and pricing a Cape-route contingency for cargo that can tolerate the extra transit time.

GM

How does that compare to the Bab-el-Mandeb situation right now?

GH

Bab-el-Mandeb is a higher-probability, lower-severity-per-incident risk right now: 63% probability that carriers keep the bulk of Asia-Europe volume on the Cape of Good Hope routing through year-end, versus Hormuz's lower-probability but higher-severity closure risk.

In practice: Hormuz risk is about a sudden stop to a critical energy flow; Bab-el-Mandeb risk is already priced into routing and mostly shows up as sustained added cost and transit time rather than a single disruptive event.
